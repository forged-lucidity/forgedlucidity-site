Read the Artifact, Not the Flag

Honest self-evaluation and recovery for autonomous robots

Gregory P. Braun  ·  Forged Lucidity, Westbrook, Maine  ·  gpb4@cornell.edu

Download the paper (PDF, 18 pp) Prepared for the CoRL 2026 Workshop on Agentic Robotics

Abstract

A robot working with no person nearby must decide for itself whether its task is done, and a self-improving robot must also decide which of its own changes to keep. Can it make those calls honestly? Not with a grader learned from its own success reports: such a grader learns to pass the failures the robot cannot sense, and some that it can.

In a simulated pick-and-place task on a model of the AgileX NERO arm, with a scripted policy, a classifier trained on the robot's reports passed 1% of planted failures when every failure in its training data had been sensed and 76% when a cube could slip unnoticed from the gripper on 30% of carries, while its agreement with the robot's own labels overstated its real accuracy. A fixed check that reads the final state from an overhead camera, in a scene built for it, passed none of the planted failures and matched ground truth on all 600 natural test episodes. The same check can drive repair: acting only on the check's image, the robot repaired 92% of its natural failures and every planted failure within reach, raising task success at the highest slip rate from 49% to 95%, and in 1,000 test episodes it never reported a failed task as done; when it could not repair a failure, it stopped and asked for help.

When the robot tunes itself, its own settings can loosen its own check. In a 50-round keep-or-kill loop, a grader retrained on the robot's reports came to pass between 34 and 46 of 100 planted failures from round 10 on, while its reported success stayed within 4 points of the truth; the same grader trained once on the outside check and frozen passed 1 of 100 throughout and let its loop undo a bad change. We distill three rules for evaluating robots that work alone, and a protocol for measuring recovery that gives no credit for the robot's own account.

Three rules for honest self-evaluation

  1. Judge the final state, not the robot's account of it. A task-complete flag or a model's "I verified X" is output of the system being judged, not evidence the property holds.
  2. Read the artifact, not the flag. Score from the logged record of what happened — an overhead image, a sensor trace, a proof kernel's verdict — never from the report of it.
  3. Fault-inject the grader before you trust it. A checker that has not refused a planted fault has not been shown to check.
